Make the data, then chart it

Six in-class activities, one or two for each chapter. Students fly planes, scoop beads, catch rulers and cut paper, and the numbers go straight onto a live chart on the projector. Each station shows example data until your class enters its own.

Part 1

Quality and DMAIC

Chapters 1–2 · 1 activity

Part 1 · Activity 1 of 1 · Chapters 1–2 · Teams of 4, 50 minutes

Paper airplane DMAIC

Each team throws its plane 5 times for the baseline, 5 times after its improvement, and 5 times when another team builds the plane from its written instructions. Did the improvement move the mean, shrink the spread, or both?

Part 2

Distributions in a candy bag

Chapter 3 · 1 activity

Part 2 · Activity 1 of 1 · Sections 3.2–3.3 · One bag per pair, 35 minutes

Candy distributions

Each pair counts the pieces in its bag, the pieces of the target color, and the defects (cracked, chipped or misprinted pieces). Is the color count binomial? Is the defect count Poisson, with mean ≈ variance?

Part 3

Chance causes and the seven tools

Chapter 5 · 2 activities

Part 3 · Activity 1 of 2 · Section 5.2 · 6 workers × 4 days, 45 minutes

Deming's Red Bead Experiment

The bowl holds 20% red beads and every paddle takes 50. The manager praises and punishes, but the np chart shows what is really going on. Type each red-bead count into the grid as the chief inspector announces it.

Part 3 · Activity 2 of 2 · Section 5.4 · Class check sheet, one week

Why are we late? A class Pareto chart

After the fishbone, each student ticks a check sheet every time they are late for one week. Enter the totals and the bars sort themselves. Which one or two causes make up most of the lateness?

Part 4

Control charts for variables and attributes

Chapters 6–7 · 2 activities

Part 4 · Activity 1 of 2 · Sections 6.2 and 5.3.4 · Pairs, 40 minutes

Ruler-drop x̄ and R charts

Each catcher makes 5 drops: one subgroup. Phase I sets the limits. In Phase II some catchers secretly use the other hand while counting backward by 7. Can the class spot them on the chart?

Enter one subgroup

x̄ chart (top) and R chart (bottom)

Part 4 · Activity 2 of 2 · Sections 7.2 and 7.3 · Teams of 3, 35 minutes

Typo hunt: c chart and p chart

Each team counts the typos on the 20 printed cards. The c chart shows typos per card. The p chart counts how many of the 85 planted typos each team found, and asks whether the inspectors agree.

Part 5

Process and measurement capability

Chapter 8 · 2 activities

Part 5 · Activity 1 of 2 · Sections 8.2–8.3 · Every student, 30 minutes

Paper strip capability

Cut strips to 100 ± 3 mm. Round 1 is by eye, Round 2 uses a marked line. Compare Cp and Cpk for the two methods: same people, different system.

Enter strip lengths

Strip lengths against the specification

Part 5 · Activity 2 of 2 · Section 8.7 · 5 strips × 3 operators × 2 readings, 15 minutes

Is the ruler good enough? A small gauge study

Three operators measure the same 5 strips twice, without seeing their first readings. Repeatability comes from the ranges of the two readings and reproducibility from the operator means. P/T compares the gauge spread with the tolerance.
