Watch the center. Watch the spread.

When a quality characteristic is measured on a scale (a width in microns, a diameter in mm, a cost in dollars), one chart watches the process mean and a second chart watches its variability. Build x̄ and R charts from the book's hard-bake data, judge capability, read patterns, size the sample, then switch to s charts and individuals charts.

It is important to maintain control over both the process mean and process variability. Montgomery, Section 6.1

Control limits are not specification limits

Control limits come from the spread of x̄, σ/√n. Natural tolerance limits are μ ± 3σ for single parts. Specification limits are set by the customer or designer. See how far apart they sit, then sort the statements.

OC curve and ARL

The mean moves by kσ. β = Φ(L − k√n) − Φ(−L − k√n) is the chance the next sample still plots inside the limits. ARL₁ = 1/(1 − β), ATS = ARL₁·h, and I = n·ARL₁ units are inspected before the signal.

x̄ and s charts
