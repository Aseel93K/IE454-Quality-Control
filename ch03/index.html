Every process varies. A model tells you how much.

No two bottles, wafers or shafts are exactly alike. First you picture the variation in your data, then you choose a probability model that describes it, then you use the model to predict how many parts will be out of spec. Try each step below with real numbers from the book.

Statistics is the science of analyzing data and drawing conclusions, taking variation in the data into account. Montgomery, Section 3.1

Part 4 · Activity 2 of 3 · Section 3.3.1, Central limit theorem · Classroom dice

Average some dice and a bell appears

One die is flat: every face has probability 1/6. Roll n dice, take the average, repeat 2,000 times. As n grows, the averages pile up in a bell shape and the spread shrinks like σ/√n.

Does the fat pencil cover the points?

Plot each sorted value x(j) against its normal score zj, where Φ(zj) = (j − 0.5)/n. If a fat pencil laid on the line covers every point, a normal model is reasonable.
