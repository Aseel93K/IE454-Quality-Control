In control is not the same as capable

A process can be perfectly stable and still make parts outside the specifications. Process capability compares the natural spread of the process (μ ± 3σ) with the specification band. Estimate it from a histogram, a probability plot or a control chart, sum it up as Cp and Cpk, and learn how far to trust the number.

The control chart should be regarded as the primary technique of process capability analysis. Montgomery, Section 8.4

Natural tolerance limits

The natural tolerance limits of a process are μ ± 3σ. For a normal process they hold 99.73% of the output, so 0.27% (2,700 parts per million) falls outside. If the output is skewed, that share can be very different.

Compute Cp and the share of the band used

Cp = (USL − LSL)/6σ compares the width of the specifications with the natural spread. P = (1/Cp)·100 is the percentage of the specification band the process uses up. Estimate σ from the R chart as R̄/d₂. These numbers assume a centered, normal, in-control process.

DPU and DPMO

With defect counts, capability is often stated as defects per unit, DPU = defects/units, or defects per million opportunities, DPMO = defects/(units × opportunities). An invoice field can be wrong or blank, so each field counts as two opportunities.
